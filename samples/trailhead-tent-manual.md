# Trailhead 2 Tent: Owner's Manual

Thank you for choosing the Trailhead 2, a two-person, three-season backpacking tent built for long days on the trail and quick setups at camp. Please read this manual before your first trip and keep it with your gear.

## Specifications

### Weights and dimensions

Minimum trail weight is 1.62 kg, which counts the tent body, rainfly, and poles. Packed weight, including stakes, guylines, and stuff sacks, is 1.89 kg.

The floor measures 218 cm long and 132 cm wide at the head end, tapering to 112 cm at the foot. Peak interior height is 102 cm. Each vestibule adds 0.9 square meters of covered storage.

### Materials

The fly is 20-denier ripstop nylon with a silicone coating on the outside and a polyurethane coating on the inside, rated to a 1,500 mm hydrostatic head. The floor uses 30-denier nylon rated to 3,000 mm. Poles are 8.7 mm aluminum alloy with color-coded tips.

## Setup

### Choosing a site

Look for flat, durable ground that is at least 60 meters from lakes and streams. Avoid low spots where cold air and rainwater collect overnight, and check overhead for dead branches. A slight slope is fine if you sleep with your head uphill.

### Pitching the body

Lay the footprint down first if you have one, then spread the body on top with the door facing away from the wind. Stake the four corners, pulling each one out at a 45-degree angle until the floor is taut but not stretched. Assemble the hubbed pole and seat the color-coded tips in the matching corner grommets, then clip the body to the pole starting at the bottom and working up. The whole process takes about four minutes once you have done it a couple of times at home.

### Attaching the fly

Drape the fly over the frame so that its colored corner webbing lines up with the same colors on the body. Buckle each corner, then fasten the hook-and-loop wraps on the underside of the fly around the poles. Tighten the corner straps evenly so the fly does not touch the inner tent anywhere; contact between the two layers lets water wick through. Stake out both vestibules and the two side guylines.

## Weather

### Wind

In gusts above 40 km/h, pitch with the narrow foot end facing into the wind and use all six guy-out points. Tie guylines to rocks or trees where stakes will not hold. Re-tension everything after the first hour, since nylon stretches as it cools and gets wet.

### Heavy rain

Keep both vestibule doors zipped and open the roof vent halfway. Do not pitch in a depression, and never dig trenches around the tent. If the ground is soaked, stakes may pull out, so check them before going to sleep.

## Care and Storage

### Cleaning

Set the tent up and sponge it off with cool water and a small amount of non-detergent soap. Never put it in a washing machine or dryer, and never use bleach, solvents, or spot removers, because they break down the waterproof coatings. Rinse well and let everything air-dry completely in the shade.

### Long-term storage

Before putting it away for the winter, make sure every part is bone dry; even a little moisture causes mildew and makes the coatings peel. Keep it loosely in a large cotton or mesh bag, not the compression sack, somewhere cool and dark. Hanging it in a closet works well. Store the poles assembled or loosely folded so the shock cord can relax.

## Troubleshooting

### Water inside the tent

Damp walls in the morning are almost always condensation, not a leak. Your breath and wet gear release moisture, and it collects on the cold fabric overnight. Open both vents, crack a vestibule door, and avoid camping right next to water. If you find drips along a seam, apply seam sealer to the inside of the fly along that seam and let it cure for 24 hours.

### Stuck zippers

Never force a stuck slider. Back it up, ease the fabric out of its teeth, and close the door from both sides with the fly taut. A small amount of zipper lubricant helps in sandy or freezing conditions. Zippers that no longer close behind the slider usually just need the slider squeezed gently with pliers.

### Pole repair

If a pole section cracks on the trail, slide the aluminum repair sleeve from the stake bag over the break and tape both ends. It will hold until you get home and order a replacement section.
